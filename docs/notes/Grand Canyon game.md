# Grand Canyon game (2026-10-10)

The next game: **Build Across the Grand Canyon!** It reuses the Plant Trees! place, its training, shop, UI and saving. Players carry planks and build a bridge across a canyon together. Decided 2026-10-10; the numbers below are starting defaults to tune.

## Decisions so far

- **Same Roblox place** as Plant Trees!. The user renames the game. Passes, products and badges stay with the game, so players who bought training passes keep them.
- **Plant Trees! code is saved** on git `main` (tag `plant-trees-final`). The canyon game is built on the `grand-canyon` branch.
- **Fresh start:** a new save store, so everyone starts at zero (passes still count).
- **Wooden planks only** for now (no rope or steel tiers).
- **Before Studio changes:** the user saves a copy of the Plant Trees! place to a file (File → Save to File As). Roblox's Version History also keeps the published versions.
- **Name check:** confirm "Build Across the Grand Canyon!" isn't already taken on Roblox.

## How it plays (proposed)

- **Your side of the canyon** is the hub: training benches and treadmills, the shop, and a **plank pile** (instead of the acorn piles).
- **The bridge** starts at your rim and has to reach the far rim. It's a grid of deck slots, 3 across.
  - Only the **front row** of the bridge is open, like the one open row in the forest.
  - Each slot takes a number of planks. The number grows with the server's head count, like seeds per tree.
  - A slot is finished when it's full. When the row is full, the next row opens further out.
- **Why Strength and Speed matter:** the bridge gets longer, so every trip from the plank pile to the front gets longer. Strength (more planks per trip) and Speed (shorter trips) are the whole progression, as in Build the Pyramid.
- **Falling:** step off the edge and you drop into the canyon and respawn at the rim (FallService already does this). Finished rows get railings.
- **Coins** for every plank placed. Upgrades and passes are bought with them, as now.
- **The finish:** when the bridge touches the far rim, everyone celebrates and gets a reward, and then the next canyon starts.
- **Rounds:** each canyon is wider than the last. Starting guesses are 300, 600, then 1,000 studs, with 10-stud slots (90, 180, then 300 slots).

## Crowding and long trips (2026-10-10)

- **Players walk through each other** (`CollisionService`), so a full server can't jam the bridge or push people off.
- **5 sections wide** (50 studs, was 30): more room and 5–10 open sections at the bridge's end. With 5 sections a full bridge is 1,500 sections, so a solo player needs about 4,500 planks at the minimum of 3 per section. Tune after playtests (`ForestSize.MinSeedsPerHex`).
- **Conveyor belts, the user's call (no supply camps):** lane 3 carries you out and lane 6 back (of 8), with scrolling arrows. Since 2026-10-10 (the user's call) each belt runs at **one flat speed** along its whole length, set from how far the bridge has got: speed = built length / `TripSeconds` (20) − `BaseWalk` (32), up to 250 studs/s. A base-speed walker reaches the end in about 20 s whether the bridge is a quarter or three quarters built; faster walkers get there sooner. The outward belt slows to a stop over the last 40 studs before the unfinished end. It used to speed up with distance from the rim. Settings in `GameConfig.Grid.Belts`. Not playtested (Studio stuck starting).
- Optional, not done: letting the end run 2 rows ahead (`Grid.AheadRings`).

## The real canyon (2026-10-10)

The terrain is built from real elevation data of the Toroweap stretch of the Grand Canyon: a sheer inner gorge between two flat benches at about 1,400 m, with the river about 760 m below.

- **Source:** nine elevation tiles (about 1.1 MB) kept in `tools/canyon_dem/`, with an attribution line for the game's description in its README.
- **Shaping** (`tools/canyon_heightmap.py`):
  - The river runs north-south, with camp on the northwest bench.
  - The gap is stretched so rim to rim along the bridge is exactly 3,000 studs (2.3 studs per real meter across).
  - The depth is 2.6 studs per meter, so about 2,000 studs to the river, with walls a bit steeper than real.
  - Rock ledges are added every 70 real meters, plus a little noise.
  - Shaped for the game: a flat camp, a sheer drop at both bridgeheads, a clear line under the bridge, a tall butte where bridges 1 and 2 end, and a flat landing on the far rim.
  - The river fills the deepest 6% of the gorge.
- **The far side** (2026-10-10, the user found the end unsatisfying: the landing was a raised rectangle and the map stopped 700 studs past the far rim):
  - The far bench (30 m lower in real life) is lifted level with camp, so the bridge lands on natural ground.
  - The landing is a round, soft-edged flat spot with a FAR RIM flag.
  - The map reaches 1,800 studs past the far rim. Beyond the first 300 studs the land is squeezed 3.5× (`BACKDROP`), so the real hills about 2.5 km back rise as a red wall of cliffs and peaks, up to about 800 studs above the rim. Ground over 50 studs above the rim is red (the Moenkopi layer).
  - The haze builds with distance.
  - Checked in a playtest: a full 3,000-stud bridge, the far landing and the finish cutscene showing the whole span.
- **Far scenery** (2026-10-10, the user: "if I look left and right the canyon just disappears"): the cause was streaming. Players' devices only load terrain within about 1,000 studs, and the terrain map ends 2,000 studs to each side.
  - The fix is 235 low-detail mesh tiles of the whole canyon, over about 13,000 × 16,000 studs, built from the same height function as the terrain (`Canyon.heights`). The canyon now runs to the horizon in every direction, including the real river bends and a volcanic cone northeast of camp.
  - They live in `Workspace.Map.FarCanyon`, which is set to always load (Persistent). They're Sandstone material, which is matte like the terrain (SmoothPlastic looked shiny, the user's note), smooth shaded (normals computed across tile edges so neighbors match; flat shading looked low-poly next to the terrain), with box collision and lower detail with distance, about 268,000 triangles in all.
  - Inside the terrain's area each tile point takes the lowest terrain height within one step around it, then sinks a little, so loaded terrain always covers the tiles.
  - **Version 2 (2026-10-10, the user: the terrain and the meshes "still don't align perfectly", and the canyon didn't seem to go on for miles):**
    - **The join:** on a player's device the terrain only loads about 1,000 studs out, and past that the tiles show in its place. Version 1's tiles there sat 12 studs low on a 32-stud grid, so their cliffs and mesas were about 32 studs shrunken and stepped down. Over the terrain's area they're now on a 16-stud grid and sink only 3 studs, back to true height within 96 studs past the terrain's edge (was 400). In a playtest from the bridge's middle the join no longer shows.
    - **Checked offline at 42,000 points:** the mesh never comes within half a stud of dry terrain. The only exceptions are under the river, where the water covers them, and 3 points on a sheer step 1,000 studs down.
    - **The distance:** the scenery now runs to 20,480 studs each way along the canyon and from x -10,240 to 8,192, with 2,048-stud outer tiles on a 128-stud grid. The elevation tiles end about 15,000 studs along the canyon; past them `canyon_heightmap.bilinear` mirrors the land (`mirror`) instead of smearing the edge pixels. The playable terrain is byte-for-byte unchanged (checked).
    - **The haze:** Atmosphere Density is 0.1 (was 0.15), so the canyon shows farther before it fades.
    - **Size:** 519 tiles and about 528,000 triangles. Flat stretches are merged in Blender (`FLAT_DEGREES`) and the engine lowers detail with distance.
    - **The road strips** past the terrain were rebuilt on the new heights.
  - Colors come from one color strip (`farfield_colors.png`, decal 96902156250519): one color per height, the terrain's exact layer colors, plateau ground and the river. The first strip (rock on one half, ground on the other, picked by slope, plus random streaks) painted streaks through the layers and muddied the colors at a distance (the user's note, 2026-10-10).
  - The haze builds with distance (Atmosphere Density 0.1, Offset 0.6, Haze 0.8).
  - **Pipeline:**
    1. `python tools/canyon_farfield.py`
    2. In Blender, `tools/blender/build_farfield.py` `build(repo)` writes `FarCanyon.fbx`.
    3. `python tools/upload_fbx.py tools/canyon_out/FarCanyon.fbx "Canyon Far Scenery"`
    4. Insert the model with the Studio connection's `insert_asset` into ServerStorage as `FarCanyonImport` (`InsertService:LoadAsset` refuses fresh uploads with "not authorized").
    5. Set `MODEL_ID` in `tools/build_farfield.luau` and run it.
  - **Gotchas:** keep every mesh under 2,048 studs (tall tiles split into four); Blender +X becomes Roblox -X. Uploaded assets: model 70525554318258 (version 3, in use; 97523121286902, 99072621223554 and 111837955958933 are older), colors image 77249289416370 (decal 96902156250519 before; 76092613884949 is the streaky first strip).
- **Building it:** run `python tools/canyon_heightmap.py`, serve the repo on port 34877, run `tools/canyon_terrain.luau` in Studio (about 10 seconds, 7.4 million voxels), then `tools/build_canyon.luau`.
- **Rock layers by height:** pale limestone at the rim, tan sandstone, red shale, red ledges, stained red limestone, a grey-green platform, and dark schist in the inner gorge. Flat tops are dusty ground and the river bank is sand.
- **Falling:** falling off the bridge sends you back to camp once you're 200 studs down (was 50).
- **Memory:** check it on a phone. Studio shows about 120 MB of terrain voxels, but it counts all its copies together. Streaming is on, so phones only load what's near them. If it's too heavy: a thinner rock shell (`SHELL` in `canyon_terrain.luau`), a smaller map (`Z_MIN`/`Z_MAX`) or a lower streaming radius.
- **Pacing:** a full bridge is now 900 sections, so a solo player needs about 2,700 planks (3 per section minimum), and trips get long. This is the "larger than life" feel; tune after playtests.
